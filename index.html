How the no-repeat works: every business you Add or Dismiss is saved on this device straight away and backed up to your account. New searches skip anything you've already actioned — including a business OpenStreetMap has re-listed under a new id, matched on name plus area, address or phone — and anything marked “Dismissed”, “Has website” or “Not interested” stays hidden for good. The line under the tabs tells you the truth about saving: ☁ synced means it's safely in your account, ☁ saving… or offline means it's on this device and still queued, and it keeps retrying until it's up.
Choosing a category: each All … option covers just the one group it sits in — “All health & wellness” won’t return a plumber. All business types at the top sweeps every group at once. It runs as several smaller searches — you’ll see a 3/6 style counter while it works — so it takes under a minute even at 40 km. Trades, food & drink, motoring and professional services are usually the biggest untapped groups in a town, and every one of them is a one-page-demo business. If a run says some parts didn’t come back, OpenStreetMap was briefly busy: search again and the gaps fill in. On the map, a numbered circle means several businesses are stacked at that spot: zoom in or tap the number to open them.
Reading the map: pin colour shows fit — green strong, amber medium, grey weaker. Tapping a pin turns it purple so you can see what you've already looked at (the popup has an undo if you tapped by mistake), and a stack turns purple once you've opened everything in it. Add or Dismiss removes the pin altogether, so whatever is still coloured is still to do. Visited pins are remembered on this device only.
Data from OpenStreetMap contributors. Coverage of small owner-run businesses varies by town — if a search looks thin, widen the radius or move up to the group’s “All …” option, and use the Maps ↗ link on each result to verify with your usual Google check. Maps ↗ looks the business up by name with the map already zoomed to its street; if Google has no listing under that name, Pin ↗ (“exact spot” in map popups) drops you on the exact coordinates so you can see what is really there.
